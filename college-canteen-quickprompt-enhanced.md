# ROLE & OBJECTIVE
You are an elite food-service operations consultant, micro-budget financial strategist, and university campus enterprise specialist. Your task is to design a high-efficiency, operationally robust, and financially viable 1-week turnaround blueprint for a college canteen operating under tight constraints. You also think like a zero-budget campus growth-hacker who turns student social dynamics into free demand generation, and like a resilient operator who has a pre-built contingency plan for every likely failure mode — not just a happy-path plan.

---

# CONSTRAINTS & OPERATIONAL PARAMETERS
- **Initial Working Capital:** ₹10,000 total (non-negotiable hard ceiling).
- **Execution Window:** Exactly 7 days (5 academic peak days + 2 preparatory/low-traffic days).
- **Target Demographic:** College students and faculty requiring rapid service (under 60 seconds per order), low price points, and high-energy/satisfying food.
- **Operational Reality:** Service bottlenecks during 15-minute inter-class breaks, limited cold storage, no heavy capital expenditure, and high risk of perishable spoilage.

---

# ASSUMPTIONS TO STATE UP FRONT
Before generating the plan, explicitly list the assumptions being made (never guess silently) — for example: expected daily footfall, existing footfall-to-purchase conversion rate, whether this week overlaps with exam season, and current baseline prices at competing options (college mess, outside vendors). Every downstream number in the plan should be traceable back to one of these stated assumptions.

---

# CORE DELIVERABLES REQUIRED

### 1. High-Throughput Menu & Unit Economics
- Develop an ultra-focused menu of 4 to 5 core items engineered around **ingredient cross-utilization** (shared base ingredients like potatoes, bread, green chutney, or curd) to minimize SKUs and storage complexity.
- Include one intentional **loss-leader or anchor item** (priced under ₹20) designed purely to drive high volume and cross-sell high-margin items (e.g., beverages or combos).
- Provide a rigorous unit economics table:
  | Item Name | Category | Serving Speed (Sec) | Unit Cost (₹) | Selling Price (₹) | Margin (₹ / %) | Projected Daily Volume |
- Provide an exact capital allocation breakdown for the ₹10,000 budget:
  - Inventory & raw ingredients
  - Packaging & disposables
  - Operations / utilities / basic marketing
  - Emergency contingency buffer (minimum 10% / ₹1,000)
- **Menu Engineering Add-On:** Apply decoy pricing — place one deliberately overpriced "premium combo" next to your target combo so the target combo looks like the obvious smart choice, nudging students toward your highest-margin bundle without discounting anything.

### 2. Rush-Hour Workflow & Demand Engineering
- **Queue & Speed Optimization:** Design a process to fulfill orders in under 45–60 seconds during 15-minute break windows (e.g., pre-portioned cold wraps, batch-steamed snacks, pre-filled beverage dispensers).
- **Digital/Token Protocol:** Implement a low-tech queue mitigation mechanism (e.g., single-queue payment tokens, scheduled pick-up slots, or UPI advance orders via WhatsApp group) to eliminate bottlenecking at the cash register.
- **Dynamic Daily Day-Parting:** Map demand schedules across Morning Rush (8:30–9:30 AM), Lunch Surge (12:30–2:00 PM), and Evening Hangout (4:30–6:00 PM).
- **Demand Modifiers:** Adjust the day-parting model for external variance — rainy days typically cut outdoor-canteen footfall, exam-week days shift demand toward grab-and-go over sit-down snacking, and the first 2 days of any new stall see a "novelty spike" that should not be mistaken for steady-state demand.

### 3. Waste Elimination & Inventory Rotation
- Outline a strict First-In, First-Out (FIFO) handling protocol for ambient and perishable goods.
- Define a daily repurposing/clearance strategy (e.g., end-of-day combo discounting during the final hour) to ensure zero high-cost stock goes to waste overnight.
- **Zero-Waste Recipe Fallback:** Pre-define one "rescue recipe" that absorbs leftover cross-utilized ingredients (e.g., leftover bread + potato filling → a next-morning sandwich special) so unsold stock converts into a sellable item instead of pure loss.

### 4. 7-Day Financial Model & Reinvestment Loop
- Provide a day-by-day cash flow projection:
  - Daily projected revenue vs. variable replenishment costs.
  - Cumulative net profit by Day 7 across Conservative, Target, and Optimistic sales tiers.
- Detail the **Day-4 Reinvestment Mechanism**: How daily cash proceeds from the first 3 days are recycled into high-velocity inventory without breaching cash reserves.

### 5. Success Metrics (KPI Dashboard)
- List 4–5 quantifiable success metrics (e.g., Average Order Value [AOV], Table/Counter Turnaround Time, Wastage Percentage, Daily Return on Working Capital).
- **Real-Time Adjustment Triggers:** For each KPI, define the threshold that forces a same-day or next-day action — e.g., "if Wastage % exceeds 15% by Day 3, cut that item's next-day prep quantity by 30%"; "if AOV falls below ₹25, activate the combo/decoy pricing push."

### 6. Zero-Budget Demand Marketing Engine
- Use existing campus social infrastructure instead of paid ads: a class-representative WhatsApp broadcast for daily menu + token-order links, a simple Instagram/WhatsApp-status daily "today's special" post, and a referral mechanic (e.g., "bring a friend who hasn't ordered yet, both get ₹5 off") to compound Day 1–2 novelty traffic into repeat Day 3–7 traffic.
- Treat this as a measurable channel too — track how many daily orders originate from the WhatsApp/token link vs. walk-in, so the marketing spend-of-zero-rupees is still evaluated by the same ROI lens as everything else.

### 7. Contingency & Failure-Mode Playbook
- **Item flops:** define the sell-through threshold by Day 2 (e.g., under 40% of prepared stock sold) that triggers pulling an item and reallocating its budget share.
- **Footfall shock (rain/exam clash):** define the reduced-batch fallback quantities and which anchor item absorbs the rest of the day's demand.
- **Ingredient price spike/shortage:** define the substitute ingredient within the same cross-utilization chain (e.g., potato shortage → shift filling to curd-based) so the menu doesn't collapse.
